1. Existing product research
Researched Gravity Sketch as an additional existing product for our project. Examined its main
strengths and weaknesses, particularly its direct manipulation approach and reliance on VR
hardware. Considered how similar interaction methods could be adapted to our webcam-based
gesture system.

2. User research
Reviewed the existing potential-user interview questions and prepared an interview draft for a
beginner-level user. Focused on understanding how users interact with 3D objects, their
preferences regarding voice and gestures, and potential concerns about accuracy, privacy and
fatigue.

3. Design research and coordination
Reviewed the existing design work in the HCI Wiki, including the accessibility settings, voice
and gesture controls, visual feedback and privacy indicators. Identified possible areas for further
contribution, particularly interaction feedback and error handling.

16.11.2026:

I have drafted a demo version of the app. It can do all of the basic 3D editor functions
mentioned in the plan: save/undo/redo, a set of given models, picking colors for the
models, and choosing the way of input (e.g. mouse & keyboard, mic only, cam only, or
mic and cam mode). In addition, it has a rotatable and zoomable 3D workspace with
objects that can be rotated and resized, and a visible record of past actions with revert
to a specific action possibility.

The demo is a web application that runs on JS, with Three.js for 3D object rendering. The
scene is saved in a JSON file (for now, this still needs to be improved). Ideally, the scene
should be saved in a format that works with other 3D editors.

That took most of the past two weeks. While developing, I also did some preliminary
research on alternative products, mainly Spline V2 and Blender MCP. Both are fairly
popular, but they take different approaches. Spline V2's AI features are behind a paywall,
while Blender MCP is open source and can also run on a self-hosted AI.
